What Ei Is

Ei is a local-first AI companion. The application runs on your device. Your data lives on your device. The developer (Jeremy Scherer, flare576@gmail.com) provides the web interface at ei.flare576.com and an optional encrypted sync service as a convenience — not as a data custodian.

Your Data Is Yours

Everything Ei learns about you is stored locally in $EI_DATA_PATH on your machine. I have no access to it. If you use the optional Sync feature, your data is encrypted on your device before it ever leaves — I store a blob I cannot read.

The web app stores your data in your browser's IndexedDB. localStorage is used only to migrate data from older sessions and as a fallback when IndexedDB isn't available. The TUI stores everything in your local filesystem. Either way: your machine, your data.

What I'm Actually Providing

Three things, all optional depending on how you use Ei:

The web interface at ei.flare576.com — a static app served to your browser. If it goes down, the TUI still works and your data is untouched.

The sync API — stores your encrypted backup between devices. If it goes down, your local copy is unaffected.

OAuth relay endpoints — used during initial app authorization (e.g. /callback/slack/tui). These are one-time flows; existing tokens are unaffected if they go down.

If I Ever Shut It Down

I'll give at least 30 days notice before discontinuing ei.flare576.com or the sync service. That's enough time to:

— Download your data via Backup & Restore (web)
— Switch to the TUI, which has no dependency on my server
— Export your sync blob before the API goes dark

The source code is open. If I disappear, someone can host it. github.com/Flare576/ei

No Warranty

Ei is provided as-is. I make no guarantees about uptime, data integrity, or fitness for any particular purpose. It's a personal project I use daily and care about — but it's not a commercial service with an SLA.

Changes to These Terms

If something significant changes, I'll update this page and note the date. Continued use after changes means you're OK with them. If you're not, your data is already on your machine — you can walk away cleanly.
